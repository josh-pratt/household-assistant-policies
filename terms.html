Household Assistant — Terms and Conditions

Last updated: 24 August 2026

Program name

Household Assistant.

Program description

Household Assistant is a private, personal text-message service operated by Thomas Joshua Pratt, a sole proprietor, for himself and adult members of his own immediate household. It sends schedule and deadline reminders to a household member, accepts replies conversationally, and sends occasional family coordination messages (for example, a reminder about a family dinner) to members of the operator's immediate family.

It is not a commercial service and carries no marketing, promotional, or third-party content. Recipients are the operator and adult members of his own immediate household, who enrol themselves through the sign-up form on the Household Assistant home page.

How to sign up

Enrolment is entirely voluntary and consent is never assumed. A person opts in by visiting the Household Assistant home page, entering their mobile number and checking a consent checkbox that is unchecked by default, then texting START to the program's number, +1 (385) 398-9120, from that same mobile phone to confirm. They then receive one confirmation message.

Consent to receive text messages is not required to use any service and is not a condition of any purchase.

Message frequency

Message frequency varies, up to 20 messages per day. Family coordination messages are sent no more than a few times per month.

Cost

Message and data rates may apply. Household Assistant does not charge recipients anything. Any charges are those of the recipient's own mobile carrier.

Opting out

Reply STOP at any time to stop all messages. Opting out is immediate and permanent until the recipient opts back in. There is no penalty and no follow-up message asking the recipient to reconsider.

The words STOP, STOPALL, UNSUBSCRIBE, CANCEL, END and QUIT all stop messages.

Getting help

Reply HELP at any time for information about the service and how to contact the operator. You can also email t.josh.pratt+sms@gmail.com.

Mobile carriers are not liable for delayed or undelivered messages.
